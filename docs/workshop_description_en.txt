[h2]Overview[/h2]
Standard of Living (SOL) is an economy and balance overhaul built for deep-strategy players. Its core goal is to make pop demand less static: local income, estate savings, market base spending, and estate-building maintenance all affect how much a location can actually consume, while development-based demand scaling and gates are disabled for clean calibration.

[h2]Core Features[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Dynamic living standard and pop demand[/b]: Recalibrates baseline pop demand for 55 goods, then applies a monthly local_pop_demand modifier to every owned land location.
[*][b]Income-closed consumption model[/b]: Local stratum income, national estate savings pressure, and market base spending combine into the final local demand coefficient. Development does not scale or gate pop demand.
[*][b]Market spending cache[/b]: Market consumed-goods data and unit-pop base spending are refreshed yearly. Spending uses the lower of market price and default price, preventing expensive goods from endlessly inflating the base.
[*][b]Estate-building maintenance[/b]: Each year, the mod counts estate buildings in each location and subtracts maintenance from the matching stratum income.
[*][b]Living Standard UI[/b]: Adds a national Living Standard situation, map coloring, location panel data, and a manual cache refresh action for inspecting demand coefficients, income, savings, maintenance, and consumed goods.
[/list]

[h2]Economic Balance[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Age economic escalation[/b]: As the game advances through the ages, building efficiency falls, RGO expansion becomes more expensive, city/town upgrades cost more, and pop food consumption rises. Later ages regain prosperity, but expansion becomes much more costly.
[*][b]Anti-snowball construction[/b]: Base RGO size is reduced, low-control locations are worse for construction, and roads/railroads are much more expensive.
[*][b]Tax and difficulty tuning[/b]: Default base tax efficiency is -15% and can be adjusted through CMF. Vanilla tax bonuses from AI Hard/Very Hard and Player Easy/Very Easy are halved.
[*][b]Diplomacy and stability investment[/b]: Diplomatic spending changes with used diplomatic capacity, while cultural tradition can reduce stability investment cost.
[*][b]GDP-to-development[/b]: Locations receive yearly development growth scaled by local GDP.
[*][b]AI recovery support[/b]: AI countries can receive a configurable devastation recovery bonus, helping negative-prosperity locations recover.
[/list]

[h2]War And Expansion[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Harsher war consequences[/b]: War exhaustion further damages levy size, control, stability, legitimacy, and defensive capability.
[*][b]Occupation pressure[/b]: Total occupation and capital occupation greatly accelerate war exhaustion.
[*][b]Wartime restriction[/b]: Optionally prevents the Reduce War Exhaustion cabinet action while at war.
[*][b]Local war damage[/b]: Blockades, sieges, occupations, looting, horde raids, and razing more strongly affect prosperity, control, construction, migration attraction, development, raw materials, and food.
[*][b]Colonial restrictions[/b]: AI generally needs more than 500 tax base to create colonial charters. Colonial nations only colonize in their capital region. Historical colonizers are exempt. Players are not restricted.
[*][b]Diplomatic action restraint[/b]: Asking another country to join a war costs more favors, reducing call-to-arms spam.
[/list]

[h2]Food, Climate, And Prices[/h2]
[list]
[*]Normal winters and raised levies create stronger local food pressure.
[*]Extreme Little Ice Age event penalties to food and output are softened to reduce pure disaster spirals.
[*]Many gold transfers, advisors, commanders, religious actions, diplomatic actions, and road prices are rebalanced. Generals, admirals, advisors, roads, and railroads now require more meaningful gold investment.
[/list]
