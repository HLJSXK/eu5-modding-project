[h1]Standard of Living - v1.1 Legacy[/h1]

[h2]v1.1 legacy version. This branch keeps the full SOL demand system.[/h2]

Standard of Living (SOL) is an economy and balance overhaul built for deep-strategy players. Its core goal is to make pop demand stop being a static vanilla table: income, social stratum, market scarcity, substitute goods, price pressure, development, war damage, and late-game expansion costs all matter.

[b]Use this version if you are playing the old v1.1 branch or want the complete pre-1.3 SOL economy. The newer 1.3 beta build is intentionally narrower because engine changes broke parts of the old demand architecture.[/b]

[h2]Core SOL Features[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Full dynamic living standard and pop demand[/b]: Replaces static vanilla pop demands with generated, income-aware demand values.
[*][b]20 substitute-goods groups[/b]: Pops can shift demand inside groups such as staple food, protein, heating fuel, household goods, clothing, luxury goods, spices, medicine, weapons, mounts, and knowledge goods.
[*][b]Market scarcity tiers[/b]: Goods that are scarce become less attractive substitutes, while cheap surplus goods absorb more demand.
[*][b]Per-stratum Engel curves[/b]: Nobles, clergy, burghers, commoners, and tribesmen do not spend like one identical average pop. Staple prices matter more to commoners; luxury prices matter more to elites.
[*][b]Market-hub scarcity correction[/b]: Each market stores scarcity pressure by stratum, so local demand reacts to the market the location actually belongs to.
[*][b]Era demand coefficient[/b]: Early ages receive a stronger demand-compensation coefficient, which gradually decays as the world economy gains more goods and building slots.
[*][b]Living Standard UI[/b]: Adds a national Living Standard situation, map coloring, location panel data, and detailed substitute-goods tooltips.
[/list]

[h2]Economic Balance[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Age economic escalation[/b]: As the game advances through the ages, buildings and RGO expansion become more expensive, city/town upgrades cost more, and pop food consumption rises. Later ages regain prosperity, but expansion becomes much more expensive.
[*][b]Anti-snowball construction[/b]: Base RGO size is reduced, low-control locations are worse for construction, and roads/railroads require much more meaningful gold investment.
[*][b]Tax and difficulty tuning[/b]: Default base tax efficiency is -15% and can be adjusted through CMF. Vanilla tax bonuses from AI Hard/Very Hard and Player Easy/Very Easy are halved.
[*][b]Diplomacy and stability investment[/b]: Diplomatic spending changes with used diplomatic capacity, while cultural tradition can reduce stability investment cost.
[*][b]GDP-to-development[/b]: Locations receive yearly development growth scaled by local GDP.
[*][b]AI recovery support[/b]: AI countries can receive a configurable devastation recovery bonus, helping negative-prosperity locations recover.
[/list]

[h2]War And Expansion[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Harsher war consequences[/b]: War exhaustion further damages levy size, control, stability, legitimacy, and defensive capability.
[*][b]Occupation pressure[/b]: Total occupation and capital occupation greatly accelerate war exhaustion.
[*][b]Wartime restriction[/b]: Optionally prevents the Reduce War Exhaustion cabinet action while at war.
[*][b]Local war damage[/b]: Blockades, sieges, occupations, looting, horde raids, and razing more strongly affect prosperity, control, construction, migration attraction, development, raw materials, and food.
[*][b]Colonial restrictions[/b]: AI generally needs more than 500 tax base to create colonial charters. Colonial nations only colonize in their capital region. Historical colonizers are exempt. Players are not restricted.
[*][b]Diplomatic action restraint[/b]: Asking another country to join a war costs more favors, reducing call-to-arms spam.
[/list]

[h2]Food, Climate, And Prices[/h2]
[list]
[*]Normal winters and raised levies create stronger local food pressure.
[*]Extreme Little Ice Age event penalties to food and output are softened to reduce pure disaster spirals.
[*]Many gold transfers, advisors, commanders, religious actions, diplomatic actions, and road prices are rebalanced. Generals, admirals, advisors, roads, and railroads now require more meaningful gold investment.
[/list]

[h2]CMF Settings[/h2]
[list]
[*][b]Use Community Mod Framework 2.* to enable in-game control of mod features.[/b]
[*]SOL pop demand master toggle and map coloring toggle.
[*]Economic balance master toggle, base tax efficiency slider, age escalation, stability investment discount, diplomatic spending adjustment, difficulty tax nerf, GDP-to-development, and AI prosperity recovery.
[*]War acceleration master toggle, higher war exhaustion, harsher war exhaustion effects, and wartime Reduce War Exhaustion restriction.
[/list]

[h2]Compatibility[/h2]
[list]
[*]This is the old v1.1 full-SOL branch. For newer game versions, check the current SOL Workshop page first.
[*]Existing saves can be loaded, but new campaigns are recommended. The economy may need time to refresh caches and stabilize market prices after loading.
[*]The full v1.1 demand model is heavier than the later reduced 1.3 beta version, because it includes substitute goods, scarcity tiers, and per-stratum Engel curves.
[*]I do not know compatibility with every other mod. Please report concrete conflicts and I will try my best to fix them.
[/list]
