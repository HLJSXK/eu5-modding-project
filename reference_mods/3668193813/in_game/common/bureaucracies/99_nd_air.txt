# National Destinies -- Aïr / Agadez bureaucracies
# Four institutions of the Tuareg Sultanate of Agadez and the Kel Tuareg
# confederations of the Aïr massif (1449 onward). The sultanate emerged
# as a kingmaker compromise: when the Itesen, Kel Owey, Kel Tagidda, and
# their associates could no longer agree on precedence, they imported
# Yunus from Istanbul-via-the-Maghreb in 1449 to be a "neutral" sultan
# whose office was meant to bind them without empowering any one of them.
# That tension -- ritual-religious sovereignty at Agadez against the real
# political power of the imajeghen drum-chiefs -- is the institutional
# backbone of every choice in this set.
#
# Author-modifier grants 1 slot; capital adds 1 = 2 total. Player picks 2
# of 4. The two paired tradeoffs (sultanate vs Cure Salée; caravan compact
# vs ineslemen tariqat) push the player to commit to either confederation
# legitimacy or merchant-clerical legitimacy as the basis of the state.

# 1. Sultanate of Agadez -- the throne installed at Agadez in 1449 with the
#    accession of Yunus, an outsider chosen by the Itesen, Kel Owey, and
#    Kel Tagidda when they could not agree on a sultan from among themselves.
#    The sultan's writ ran in Agadez itself, in the salt assembly at In-Gall,
#    and in the tribute due from the dependent Hausa towns of Adar and Gobir;
#    his power outside the walls of the capital was always borrowed from the
#    confederations that elected him. The office gave the Aïr a fixed point
#    of Islamic legitimacy and a counterparty for foreign princes, but every
#    sultan who tried to act on his own authority was deposed.

# 2. Cure Salée Compact -- the annual congress at the salt-bearing wells of
#    In-Gall and the Tegidda-n-Tesemt salt flats, where the Kel Tuareg
#    confederations gathered at the end of the rains to water their herds,
#    settle disputes, marry across drum-groups, and confirm the sultan in
#    his office. The festival was the working constitution of the Aïr: each
#    drum-chief brought tribute of salt, dates, and animals, and each took
#    away the right to migrate north into the Tamesna plains for the dry
#    season. No sultan could ignore the Cure Salée; no ineslemen tribe could
#    escape its tariffs. The compact entrenches itself: the longer it runs
#    the harder it is to set aside, and pulling out wrecks the migrations.
