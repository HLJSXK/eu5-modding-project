# National Destinies -- Songhai Empire bureaucracies
# Six historically attested institutions of the imperial Songhai state
# (1464 Sunni Ali -> 1591 Tondibi). DELIBERATELY distinct from MAL: the
# Mali bureaucracies cover the Gbara assembly, dyamana federalism, the
# Sankore complex, the Wangara diaspora, the niamakala castes, and the
# ton-tigi cavalry. Songhai's institutional signature is different and
# later: the river-fleet office, Askia Muhammad's appointed farmas
# replacing tribal chiefs, the al-Maghili-inspired inquisitor court,
# the Tarikh chronicle scribal class, the standing slave-soldier corps
# of the Askias, and the royal-domain plantations of the Niger bend.
# Auto-modifier grants 2 slots; capital adds 1 = 3 total. Player picks
# 3 of 6.

# 1. Hi-Koy Sea-Master Office -- the admiralty of the Niger river fleet,
#    the most distinctive Songhai office and one without precedent in
#    Mali. The Hi-Koy commanded a flotilla of armoured war-canoes that
#    carried the Askia's troops up and down the inland delta from Gao
#    to Jenne, transported salt and grain to the imperial markets, and
#    enforced toll collection at the river crossings. The position
#    coexisted uneasily with the cavalry nobility: the river fleet was
#    built around fishing castes (Sorko, Bozo, Somono) whom the cavalry
#    despised but on whom imperial logistics depended.

# 3. Mori-Koyra Inquisitor Court -- on his return from the 1496-97 hajj,
#    Askia Muhammad consulted the North African scholar Muhammad al-Maghili,
#    whose Replies to the Questions of the Amir of Songhai prescribed a
#    rigorous purge of "mixed" Islamic practice and the office of an
#    inquisitor (mori-koyra, "great scholar") empowered to pursue
#    apostasy, syncretism with Bori and Hauka spirit cults, and noble
#    misuse of the law. The institution gave the imperial throne a
#    religious-judicial reach into the countryside that the nobility
#    could not block, but it inflamed the still-pagan rural majority

# 4. Tarikh Chronicle Scribes -- the literate Soninke-Sanhaja-Songhai
#    scribal class who staffed the Askias' chancery and from whose ranks
#    came the chroniclers of the Tarikh as-Sudan (Abd al-Rahman al-Sa'di,
#    Timbuktu, finished 1655) and the Tarikh al-Fattash (the Kati family,
#    begun under Askia Muhammad and continued for over a century). Unlike
#    the oral djeli tradition of Manden, this was a documentary
#    bureaucracy: appointment letters, tax registers, jurisprudential
#    fatwas, and the genealogies that legitimised the Askia line. The

# 5. Rumde Standing Corps -- the Askia's standing army of war-captives
