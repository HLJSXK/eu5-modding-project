# =====================================================================
# Khalkha Tüsheet Confederation
#
# After Dayan Khan's reunification (c.1500-1517) the Khalkha realm of
# eastern Mongolia crystallized into three hereditary khanates --
# Tüsheet Khan (centre), Sain Noyon Khan (west, separated from Tüsheet
# in 1725), and Zasagt Khan (far west). Each khan was a Chinggisid
# descendant ruling his own otogs and khoshuus, but the three sat
# together at the Khüree Chuulgan (the Khalkha grand assembly) to
# decide war, succession disputes, and tribute pacts. The confederation
# was a structural compromise between Chinggisid universal-khan claims
# and the Borjigin princes' insistence on patrimonial autonomy --
# closer to the early Mongol quriltai than to the centralized Yuan.
# =====================================================================

# =====================================================================
# Khalkha Jirum -- the 1709 codified Mongolian law
#
# Compiled between roughly 1709 and 1770 by the Khalkha grand assembly
# (Khüree Chuulgan) at the order of the Tüsheet Khan and the second
# Jebtsundamba Khutuktu, the Khalkha Jirum was the standing written
# legal code of the Khalkha confederation. Its 25 chapters integrated
# Chinggis Khan's Yassa, Altan Khan's 1577 Buddhist edicts, the
# Oirat-Khalkha 1640 Pact (the Ikh Tsaaz), and the Manchu-imposed
# Mongol-Oirat regulations of 1640 and 1696. Cases were tried before
# panels of zaisangs (clan judges) under the law's written articles
# rather than the older custom-and-precedent of separate otogs. The
# Khalkha Jirum endured as the everyday civil and criminal code of
# Mongolia until the Manchu codifications of the 1780s superseded it.
# =====================================================================

# =====================================================================
# Otog-Khoshuu Banner Administration
#
# The post-Yuan Mongol polity organised its commoners into otogs
# (decimal-pastoral kin units of nominally 1,000 households) headed
# by appointed taishis. Dayan Khan's settlement (c.1500-1517) bound
# the otogs to the six tümens, and the Khalkha princely lines turned
# them into hereditary khoshuus -- standing banners of pastoral
# households tied to a single noyan's household register. Each
# khoshuu had its own pasture rotations, postal duty (örtöö), and
# muster lists; transfer between khoshuus was forbidden and the
# noyan's word governed every commoner's movement. The Manchu Lifan
# Yuan inherited the system intact in 1691, fixed boundaries, and
# made the khoshuu the durable administrative unit of the steppe.
# =====================================================================

# =====================================================================
# Yellow Hat Sangha Patronage
#
# In 1578 Altan Khan of the Tümed met Sonam Gyatso at the Yanghua
# Monastery in Kokenuur. Altan bestowed the title Dalai Lama (the
# third) on Sonam Gyatso; Sonam Gyatso retroactively recognised the
# first two Dalai Lamas and proclaimed Altan a reincarnation of
# Khubilai Khan. The compact made the Mongol khans the temporal
# patrons of the Yellow Hat (Gelug) order and gave the Gelug a
# mounted military patron against rival Tibetan schools. The
# patronage was renewed by Abadai Khan's 1585 founding of Erdene
# Zuu on the ruins of Karakorum, and by Güshi Khan's 1642 conquest
# of Tibet on the Fifth Dalai Lama's behalf. Monasteries were
# granted shabinar (lay servant-households) exempt from princely
# taxation -- a fiscal privilege that drained the noyans and built
# Mongolia's monastic economy from the late 16th century onward.
