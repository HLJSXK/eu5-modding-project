# National Destinies -- Mexico bureaucracies

# Mexico's institutional landscape from the Plan de Iguala (1821) through
# La Reforma (1855) was dense and contradictory: the new republic inherited
# the entire Bourbon viceregal apparatus -- ecclesiastical fueros, the
# Hacienda land regime, segregated indios republics, the Audiencia courts --
# and grafted onto it the unstable post-1821 caudillo politics, the
# 1824 federal Constitution, and the centralist Siete Leyes (1836).
# Liberals and conservatives spent four decades fighting over which of
# these institutions to dismantle. Six bureaucracies capture the major
# choices that shaped that struggle.

# 1. Plan de Iguala / Ejercito Trigarante -- Iturbide's 1821 Three Guarantees

#    casta troops. The Trigarante army marched into Mexico City on 27 Sept

# 2. Iglesia Patronato y Fueros Eclesiasticos -- the new republic
#    inherited the Real Patronato of the Spanish crown: the right
#    to nominate bishops, collect tithes, and try clerics in church
#    courts under the fuero eclesiastico. The Mexican church kept
#    Bourbon-era estates (mortmain) and lent capital to the state at
#    favourable rates. Liberals like Jose Maria Luis Mora began
#    calling for fuero abolition in 1833; conservatives defended it
#    as the spine of social order. Sustained, the Patronato gives
#    the state a docile clergy, deep credit, and disciplined rural

# 4. Hacienda Latifundia -- the great rural estate, inherited from the
