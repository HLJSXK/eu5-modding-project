# =====================================================================
# Chatusadom -- the Four Pillar Ministries (Wiang, Wang, Khlang, Na)
#
# King Trailokanat's Palatine Law of 1454 reorganized Ayutthaya around
# four central ministries: Wiang (the city / capital constabulary),
# Wang (the royal palace and household), Khlang (the treasury and
# foreign trade), and Na (agriculture, irrigation, granaries). Together
# they replaced the older Sukhothai-era pattern of regional muang lords,
# pulling administrative power out of the provinces and into the king's
# personal departments at the capital. The Chatusadom is the structural
# spine of every later Thai government, surviving through Thonburi and
# Rattanakosin until the Chakri Reformation of 1892.

# =====================================================================
# Phra Khlang Sinkha -- the Royal Trade Monopoly
#
# Within the Khlang ministry, the Phra Khlang Sinkha (Department of
# Royal Warehouses) ran Ayutthaya's foreign trade as a state monopoly:
# Chinese junks called at the Khlang's wharves, the king's agents fixed
# prices on tin, sappan-wood, deer-hides, and pepper, and European
# chartered companies negotiated through the Phra Khlang's office --
# never directly with private merchants. Constantine Phaulkon, the Greek
# adventurer who rose to head the Phra Khlang under King Narai
# (r. 1656-1688), made the office briefly the most powerful in the
# kingdom. The monopoly's wealth funded the court but starved private
# Thai merchants and routinely strangled foreign relations when
# customs-officers overreached.

# =====================================================================
# Sakdina -- the Rank-Grade Registry
#
# Codified by Trailokanat in 1454, the Sakdina ("field power") system
# assigned every subject a numerical rank measured in rai of rice-land,
# from a slave's 5 sakdina, through a phrai's 25, up to a senior
# minister's 10,000 and the Maha Uparat's 100,000. The number set
# corvee obligations, court precedence, the size of one's house,
# the colour of one's robes, and the punishments one received under
# the Three Seal Law. The Krom registers tracking every man's rank
# and his department of obligation were the most invasive piece of
# pre-modern administration in mainland Southeast Asia. Rama V
# formally abolished the system in 1932, six and a half centuries
# after its formal codification.
# =====================================================================

# =====================================================================
# Phrai Suay -- the Tribute-Peasant Rolls
#
# Most Thai phrai owed corvee labour to the throne (Phrai Luang) or
# their patron lord (Phrai Som). Hill peoples and frontier minorities
# instead paid Suay -- in-kind tribute of forest products: sappanwood,
# elephant ivory, eaglewood, cardamom, tin, beeswax, gold dust. The
# Krom rolls assigned each upland community a quota and a Thai
# patron-officer to collect it. The Suay system extracted enormous
# wealth from the highlands while keeping non-Thai peoples nominally
# subjects of Ayutthaya without integrating them as Thai. It also
# bred chronic rebellions in Lan Na, the Khorat plateau, and the
# Malay sultanates of the south.

# =====================================================================
# Maha Sangharaja -- the Buddhist Hierarchy
#
# The Maha Sangharaja (Supreme Patriarch) heads the Thai Sangha and is
# appointed by the king. Beneath him, royally-chartered monasteries
# graded their monks through the nine Pali Pariyatti examinations on
# the Tipitaka -- the same examinations that produced the literate
# civil-religious class which staffed the lower bureaucracy. Royal
# patronage of monastery construction, ordinations, and Buddha-image
# casting was the single largest expense of the Ayutthayan and
# Rattanakosin courts, and the Sangharaja's purification councils
# (most famously Rama I's 1788 council) repeatedly redefined orthodox
# practice across the kingdom.
# =====================================================================

# =====================================================================
# Three Seal Law -- the Trailok / Rama I Legal Code
#
# The Kotmai Tra Sam Duang ("Law of the Three Seals") is the bureaucratic
# backbone of Thai justice. Drafted under Trailokanat in the 1450s as
# the Phra Aiyakan, devastated when the Burmese sacked Ayutthaya in
# 1767, and reconstituted in 1805 by Rama I from surviving fragments
# stamped with the seals of the Khlang, Mahatthai, and Kalahom, it
# defines criminal procedure, property law, the Sakdina rank
# punishments, and the powers of the four pillar ministers. The code
# made justice a function of the throne rather than of customary local
# arbitration, sharply curtailing noble jurisdiction over commoners.
