# 1. Mahajan Panchayat -- the Banya, Lohana, and Vora trader-guild courts of
#    Cambay, Surat, Patan, and Ahmedabad. Five-elder mahajan councils
#    adjudicated commercial disputes through customary law (mercantile riti),
#    enforced caste-guild discipline, and underwrote the bills of exchange
#    (hundi) that moved capital across the Indian Ocean. The Sultanate
#    recognized mahajan jurisdiction in commercial matters and the Mughal
#    diwans of Gujarat continued the recognition; in return the mahajans

# 2. Khambhat Bandar Customs -- the Mutasaddi-i-Bandar's office, the chief

#    Malabar, and silk from China that passed through the Gulf of Khambhat.

# 3. Pal Sea-Protection Levy -- the coastal sea-protection tax (pal or
#    pancham) paid by Gujarati merchants and Kathiawar coastal communities
#    to the Sultanate fleet for escort against Malabar pirates and, after
#    1500, against Portuguese cruisers operating from Diu and Goa. The
#    Sultanate's standing dhow-and-ghurab navy was funded almost entirely
#    from pal collections. The levy was hated where collected -- coastal
#    villages bore it disproportionately -- but it built the only Indian
#    Ocean state navy that fought the Portuguese on roughly equal terms

# 6. Hindu Darbari Brahmin Council -- the distinctive Gujarati practice of

#    from the Nagar Brahmins of Vadnagar and the Audich Brahmins of north
