# National Destinies -- Bosporan Kingdom (TDR) bureaucracies
# Three historically grounded institutions of the Greco-Gothic Crimean
# rump-state (Theodoro/Mangup successor with Bosporan revivalist trappings).
# Auto-modifier nd_tdr_bureaucracy_authority grants 1 slot; capital adds 1
# = 2 total. Player picks 2 of 3.

# Spans the Komnenoid palace chancery (the Gabras/Tzamblakon court of
# Mangup styling itself a small-scale basileia after the late Komnenoi),
# the cliff-monastery synod of the rock-cut Pontic Orthodox network
# (Inkerman, Mangup, Eski-Kermen, Chufut-Kale), and the Tauric customs
# house regulating the Greek-Italian-Tatar emporia of Cherson, Caffa and
# Soldaia.

# 1. Komnenoid Chancery -- the household chancery of the lords of Mangup,
#    who from the late fourteenth century styled themselves Komnenodoukai
#    in conscious emulation of the Trapezuntine and Constantinopolitan
#    courts. The chancery dispensed Byzantine titulature -- sebastos,
#    protostrator, eparch, megas doux -- to retainers and steppe
#    intermediaries, drafted Greek charters in the chancery hand of the
#    late Palaiologan Empire, and conducted the marriage diplomacy that
#    tied the small Crimean court to Trebizond, Wallachia, Moscow and the
#    Komnenian collateral lines. The dignity inflated faster than the
#    polity grew, but it bound steppe and city allies into a single
#    ceremonial pyramid descending from the Mangup throne.

# 2. Cliff-Monastery Synod -- the Pontic Orthodox spiritual establishment

#    refuges and scriptoria descending from the Justinianic foundations
#    of the sixth century. The synod arbitrated parish discipline,
#    licensed the icon-painting workshops of the Crimean school

# 3. Tauric Customs House -- the port-customs apparatus regulating the
