# National Destinies -- Tawantinsuyu (Inca Empire) bureaucracies
# Seven historically-grounded institutions of the Inca state, from the
# Pachacuti expansion (1438) through the Vilcabamba resistance (1572).
# Auto-modifier grants 2 slots; capital adds 1 = 3 total. Player picks
# 3 of 7. Each bureaucracy reflects a distinct face of Inca governance:
# the divine Sapa Inca and his panaqa-clan system, the four-suyu
# governorate, the quipu record-keepers, the mit'a rotational corvee,
# the mitma colonist transfers, the chosen-women's house, and the
# Qhapaq Nan road-and-tampu network.

# 1. Sapa Inca and the Panaqa System -- the Inca ruler was divine son of
#    Inti the sun, and on his death his household, mummy, lands, and
#    palaces became a panaqa, a royal corporation tasked with maintaining
#    his cult forever. Each new Sapa Inca had to build his own estate
#    from scratch because his predecessor's wealth was locked into the
#    deceased's panaqa. By the late empire under Huayna Capac and
#    Huascar (c. 1493-1532), the eleven existing panaqas of the Cuzco
#    valley collectively held more land than the reigning emperor.

	implementation_price = price:implement_bureaucracy_price

# 3. Quipucamayoc Record-Keepers -- the empire ran without writing on

# 5. Mitma Resettlement System -- when a region resisted incorporation
#    or held strategic value, the Inca state moved entire populations.
#    Loyal Quechua-speaking ayllus were transplanted into the conquered
#    territory and the conquered population, in turn, broken apart and
#    settled across the empire. The mitma simultaneously diluted local
#    cohesion, spread the imperial language, and gave rebellious zones
#    a counterweight of crown-loyal settlers. It was the most aggressive
#    instrument of Andean state-formation and it left a permanent
