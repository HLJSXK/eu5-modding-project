# Six historically-grounded institutions of the Mali Empire, from Sundiata's
# 1235 victory at Kirina and the Kurukan Fuga oath through Mansa Musa's hajj
# (1324) and the Sankore golden age into the slow fragmentation that ended
# with Songhai conquest (1591) and the final Manden retreat to Kangaba
# (c.1670). Auto-modifier grants 2 slots; capital adds 1 = 3 total. Player
# picks 3 of 6. Each bureaucracy reflects a distinct face of Manden governance:
# the constitutional Gbara assembly, the dyamana provincial governors, the
# Sankore scholarly complex, the Wangara trading diaspora, the niamakala
# caste corporations, and the ton-tigi cavalry order.

#    1236, where 29 (later 32) seats represented the Mandinka clan

#    lolou (guardians of the faith) for Islamic counsel, 4 nyamakala caste

#    speech and historical record, plus the Kamara, Konde, and Kouyate

# 3. Sankore Madrasa Complex -- by the 14th century Timbuktu housed three
#    great congregational madrasas: the Djinguereber, built in 1327 under
#    Mansa Musa to a design by the Andalusian Es-Saheli; the Sidi Yahya,
#    founded c.1440; and the older Sankore mosque (c.12th century) which
#    matured into a centre for Maliki jurisprudence, mathematics, astronomy,
#    and grammar. At its height the complex hosted some 25,000 students and
#    a class of black-robed jurists -- the ulama of Timbuktu -- whose fatawa
