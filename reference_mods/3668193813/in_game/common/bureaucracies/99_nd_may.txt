###############################################################
# National Destinies -- MAY (Maya) bureaucracies
#
# Six historically grounded institutions of pan-Maya governance,
# spanning the Yucatec Cuchcabales, the highland K'iche' kingdom,
# the post-Mayapan confederations, the Itza of Tayasal, and the
# Ah Kin priestly hierarchy. Auto-modifier grants 3 slots; the
# capital adds 1 = 4 effective. Player picks 4 of 6.
#
# 1. Halach Uinik           -- Yucatec true-man and the Batab town-headmen
# 2. Holpop Council         -- "those who sit on the mat", elder-council
# 3. Ah Kin Priesthood      -- calendar-keepers, Chilam diviners, forest-priests
# 4. Kaxan'al Codex Network -- hieroglyphic scribes and the four codices
# 5. Mayapan League         -- the eight Cuchcabal great houses in council
# 6. Itza Tribute Flotilla  -- lacustrine tribute-network of Tayasal
#
# All IDs are tag-prefixed (may_) to avoid id collisions.
###############################################################


# =====================================================================
# 1. Halach Uinik -- the Yucatec head-of-state, "true man"
#
# In the Postclassic Yucatec polities (Mani under the Xiu, Sotuta under
# the Cocom, Mayapan after 1200), the Halach Uinik held supreme
# authority -- judicial, military, and ritual -- over a Cuchcabal of
# Batab town-headmen. The office was hereditary within a noble
# patrilineage (the Cocom traced descent from Hunac Ceel) and Diego de
# Landa's Relacion (1566) records Halach Uinik appointing Batabs to
# subordinate towns and adjudicating disputes between them. Centralizing
# the Cuchcabal under a Halach Uinik concentrates noble loyalty and
# legitimacy at the cost of the autonomous town councils.
# =====================================================================

# =====================================================================
# 2. Holpop Council -- "those who sit on the mat"
#
# The Holpop ("head of the mat") in Yucatec towns convened the popolna
# council-house where Batabs and their elder advisors deliberated.
# Above the town level, Halach Uinik called the same kind of council
# from his subordinate Batabs. The Holpop held the popol -- the woven
# mat of office -- and the council balanced the Halach Uinik's
# authority through customary right of consultation. K'iche' Q'umarkaj
# (the Popol Vuh's "place of reeds") preserved the same institution as
# the council of the four hereditary lineages (the Kaweq, Nijaib',
# Sakik, and Tamub'). A working Holpop empowers the noble councillors;

# =====================================================================
# 3. Ah Kin Priesthood -- the calendar-keepers of the Long Count
#
# The Ah Kin ("he of the sun") were the hierarchical priesthood of the
# Yucatec polities. Under the Ah Kin Mai (high priest of Mayapan, last

# (oracular diviners who delivered prophecies, like the Chilam Balam
# books of Chumayel and Tizimin), the Nakom (sacrificers), the Chac
# (rain-priests), and the Ah K'in K'ax forest-priests who tended the
# wild groves. They kept the 260-day Tzolkin and 365-day Haab counts,

# =====================================================================
# 4. Kaxan'al Codex Network -- the hieroglyphic scribes
#
# The Ah Tz'ib ("he of writing") were the literate scribal class of the
# Maya polities, working with bark-paper screen-fold codices. Four
# pre-Conquest codices survive (Dresden, Madrid, Paris, and the Maya
# Codex of Mexico, formerly the Grolier) -- Diego de Landa burned the
# rest at Mani in 1562. The codices recorded astronomical tables,
# divinatory almanacs, ritual schedules, and dynastic histories. The
# scribal network was tied to the Ah Kin priesthood but distinct: a
# mathematical-archival profession with its own lineage workshops.
# Reviving it produces literate administrators who serve the throne
# over the hereditary lords.

# =====================================================================
# 5. Mayapan League -- the eight great houses in confederation
#
# From around 1200 to 1441, the city of Mayapan housed the joint
# council of the Cuchcabales -- the great houses of the Cocom (the
# ruling lineage), the Xiu (driven from Uxmal), the Itza (driven from
# Chichen), the Cupul, the Chel, the Pech, the Ah Canul, and the
# Tutul-Xiu. The Halach Uinik of each house was required to reside at
# Mayapan, a system the Spanish observers compared to a hostage league.
# The Xiu revolt of 1441 ended the league, scattering the houses into
# the sixteen post-Mayapan Cuchcabales of the contact period. Reviving
# the league re-binds the noble houses but at the cost of crown
# autonomy -- the Halach Uinik must rule by consensus of his peers.
