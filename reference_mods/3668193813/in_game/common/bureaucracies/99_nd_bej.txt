# National Destinies -- Beja bureaucracies
# 4 historically-grounded institutions of the Beja Red Sea / Eastern Desert
# polity. Auto-modifier grants 2 slots; capital adds 1 = 3 effective. Player
# picks 3 of 4. Spans the silif customary law administered by clan elders,
# the Hadariba shared-customs wardenship at the Red Sea ports, the Wadi
# Allaqi gold concession that linked Beja chiefs with Mamluk-era buyers, and
# the nazirate-of-the-dur tributary structure adapted from the Funj Sennar
# administrative model.

# 1. Silif Council -- the codified Beja customary law (the Hadendowa salif)
#    administered by clan elders sitting in assembly. The silif regulated
#    grazing rights, blood feuds, marriage contracts, and trade disputes;
#    its judgments carried the weight of ancestral authority. Every Beja
#    individual's accountability ran upward through head of family to clan
#    sheikh to tribal nazir, with each tier responsible for the conduct of
#    the tier below. The strength of the silif simultaneously stabilized the
#    pastoral order and limited any central ruler's reach.

	implementation_price = price:implement_bureaucracy_price
	maintenance_price = price:maintain_bureaucracy_price
	removal_price = price:remove_bureaucracy_price
	maintenance_price_modifier = {
		value = country_economical_base
		multiply = 0.003
	}

	potential = {

		has_game_rule = nd_bureaucracies_enabled
	}

	allow = {}

	estates_that_like = {
		tribes_estate
	}
	estates_that_dislike = {
		crown_estate

	positive_modifier = {

	}
}

# 2. Hadariba Wardenship -- the customs-sharing pact by which the Hadariba
#    Beja clans guarded the caravan roads and the Red Sea entrepots, taking
#    a fixed share of the dues collected at Aydhab (until its destruction
#    by Sultan Barsbay in 1426) and afterward at Suakin. By the time Ibn
#    Battuta visited Suakin in 1332 a Hadariba sultan ruled the mainland
#    half of the port and split the customs with the seaward overlord;
#    later the Hadariba broke the gold-trade monopoly and turned the
#    eastward route through Suakin into the leading channel.

#    through the late Mamluk period. The concession brought hard money to
#    the chiefs and tax revenue to the central treasury, but constant
#    Mamluk and later Ottoman pressure to monopolize the mines kept the

# 4. Nazirate of the Dur -- the system of nazirs governing tribal homelands
#    (each one termed a dar; pl., dur) adapted from the Funj Sennar model
#    that came to define eastern Sudanese administration after the Funj
#    rise in 1504. Each nazir held customary-law judicial authority within
#    his dar, paid tribute to the central treasury, and raised levies from
#    his tribe; the institution gave the Beja state a workable framework
#    for governing pastoralist populations across enormous distances at
#    the cost of perpetually devolving real power onto tribal grandees.
