# National Destinies -- Mazovian bureaucracies
# 5 institutions of the Piast cadet duchy of Mazovia (Plock-Czersk-Warsaw,
# 1138-1526). Auto-modifier grants 2 slots; capital adds 1 = 3 effective.
# Player picks 3 of 5.
#
# Scope rationale: MAZ is Level 1 but its institutional record is
# unusually well-documented for a cadet principality -- ducal statutes
# (1377, 1390), the earliest Mazovian land-court books (1414+), the
# Plock cathedral chapter founded c.1087, the Piast ducal chancery that
# Janusz I moved from Czersk to Warsaw in 1413, and the szlachta
# sejmiki of Czersk and Warsaw that ratified the 1414 privilege at Nowe
# Miasto. Five distinct named institutions across crown, nobles, clergy,
# and burghers axes warrant the full slate.

# 1. Statuty mazowieckie -- the criminal-procedural code Siemowit III
#    promulgated at Sochaczew on 27 April 1377 and revised in 1390. The
#    statutes regulated wergeld for the murder of a wlodyka or a
#    well-to-do peasant, codified the conditions of feud, defined rape
#    and persistent thievery, and bound the duke's courts to written
#    procedure. Mazovia's adoption of statute law half a century before
#    the Crown's own systematic codifications gave the cadet duchy a
#    legal authority disproportionate to its territory.

# 4. Plock Cathedral Chapter -- the canonical college serving the

#    to a fully constituted chapter under metropolitan Gniezno. By
#    Conrad of Mazovia's 1239 charter the chapter co-administered some
#    240 villae and twenty praedia of episcopal land; the canons
#    supplied the duchy's literate chancery clerks, kept the bishopric
