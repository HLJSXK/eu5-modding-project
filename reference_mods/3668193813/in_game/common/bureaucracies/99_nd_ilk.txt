###############################################################
# National Destinies -- ILK (Ilkhanate) bureaucracies
#
# Six historically grounded institutions of the Mongol Ilkhanate
# (1256-1335) and its Persianate-Mongol institutional fusion.
# The auto-modifier grants 2 slots; the capital adds 1 = 3
# effective. Player picks 3 of 6.
#
# 1. Yarghu (Mongol customary-law tribunal under yarghuchi judges)
# 2. Sahib-Divan (Persian chief-vizierate; Rashid al-Din)
# 3. Ghazan's Tax Cadaster (1295-1304 land census and reform)
# 4. Diwan al-Mazalim (Ghazan's mixed-jurisdiction grievance court)
# 5. Maragha Observatory (Hulagu/Tusi astronomical complex, 1259)
# 6. Keshig (palace household-guard and bureaucratic seedbed)
#
# All IDs are tag-prefixed (ilk_) to avoid id collisions.
###############################################################


# =====================================================================
# 1. Yarghu -- the Mongol court of customary law (yasa / toru), attached
#    to the royal court and presided over by yarghuchi judges initially
#    drawn from Mongol princes and noyans, later from Persian bureaucrats.
#    The yarghu adjudicated disputes among Mongols and between Mongols
#    and subject officials. Its rulings invoked Chinggisid law and the
#    qanun-e rasti (yarghu-nama), which the Ilkhans equated with
#    sharia in legitimating discourse but practiced as a parallel system.
# =====================================================================

	}

	allow = {}

		nobles_estate

	}

# =====================================================================
# 2. Sahib-Divan -- the Persian chief-vizierate that directed the civilian
#    bureaucracy of the Ilkhanate, coordinating departments for finance

#    leveraged Sasanian and Abbasid administrative traditions. Rashid

# =====================================================================
# 3. Ghazan's Tax Cadaster -- the great fiscal reform of 1295-1304, in
#    which Ghazan Khan and his vizier Rashid al-Din ordered a land census,
#    standardized tax rates by district, abolished the worst nomadic
#    exactions and tax-farming abuses, and issued bronze yarligh tablets
#    fixing each subject's obligations. The reform reversed the fiscal
#    collapse of the Arghun-Gaykhatu years (the 1294 paper-money fiasco)
#    and became the institutional template that later Persian dynasties
#    -- Jalayirid, Timurid, Safavid -- inherited.

# =====================================================================
# 5. Maragha Observatory -- the great rasadkhana founded in 1259 under
#    Hulagu Khan and directed by Nasir al-Din al-Tusi, who chose the
#    site near Lake Urmia and oversaw its construction over five years.
#    The complex housed a library of 400,000 volumes, instruments cast
#    in its on-site foundry, dormitories for resident astronomers, and
#    an endowment of which Tusi himself was administrator. Tusi, Mu'ayyad
#    al-Din al-Urdi, Qutb al-Din Shirazi and others compiled the Zij-i
#    Ilkhani (1271) and produced the Tusi-couple, foundations for later
#    observatories at Samarkand, Istanbul and Jaipur.
