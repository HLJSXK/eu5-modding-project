# 1. Missi Dominici -- royal envoys ("lord's emissaries") sent in pairs (one
#    cleric, one layman) on circuits called missatica to inspect counts,
#    bishops, abbots, and the king's own agents. The institution was
#    enlarged by Charlemagne's Capitulare missorum generale of 802 and was
#    the central instrument by which Aachen reached into distant counties.
#    Missi heard appeals, audited fiscal estates, promulgated capitularies,
#    and reported back to the palace. The system collapsed with Carolingian
#    central authority but its memory shaped every later Lotharingian
#    revival of royal supervision.

# 3. Reichskirche -- the imperial church system codified by Otto I after
#    his suppression of the Liudolfing revolts. The crown endowed
#    bishoprics and royal abbeys with comital rights, market regalia, and
#    immunities, and reserved the right to invest each new bishop with
#    ring and staff. Because celibate prelates could not found dynasties,
#    their offices reverted to the crown on death -- a structural
#    counterweight to the territorial dukes. The system anchored Ottonian
#    and Salian government until the Investiture Controversy and the
#    Concordat of Worms in 1122 prised episcopal appointments from
#    imperial control.

# 4. Kirchenvogtei -- the lay-advocacy office that bound a magnate to
#    defend a monastery or cathedral chapter in arms, hold its courts of

# 6. Treuga Dei -- the Truce of God, introduced into Germany at the synod
#    convened by Bishop Henry of Liège in 1082 and extended to the whole
#    empire by the synod of Mainz in 1085 in the presence of Henry IV.
#    The Truce forbade private warfare from sundown Wednesday to sunrise
#    Monday, on church feasts, and during Lent and Advent; the Peace of
#    God (Pax Dei) added immunity from violence for unarmed clergy,
#    peasants at the plough, and merchants on the road. Bishops enforced
#    the Truce with diocesan tribunals and the threat of excommunication.
#    Where the Treuga held, peasants could plough and merchants could
#    travel; where it broke down, the great fairs and the harvest both

# 7. Sauf-Conduit des Foires -- the safe-conduct of the fairs. Beginning
#    with Count Thibaut II of Champagne and confirmed by Philip Augustus

#    protection every merchant travelling to or from the great cycle of
#    six fairs at Lagny, Bar-sur-Aube, Provins, and Troyes. Lotharingian
