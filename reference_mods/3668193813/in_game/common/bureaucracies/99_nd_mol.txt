# National Destinies -- Moldavian bureaucracies
# 6 institutions of the Principality of Moldavia (~1346-1859).
# Auto-modifier grants 2 slots; capital adds 1 = 3 effective.
# Player picks 3 of 6.
#
# Scope rationale: MOL is Level 2 with an unusually rich documentary
# record across five centuries. The Sfatul Domnesc (great-boyar council
# attested in Stephen the Great's 110-member roster), the Mitropolia
# of Moldavia recognised at Suceava in 1401, the razesi free villages
# Stephen levied as oastea cea mica, the printed Pravila of Vasile
# Lupu (Iasi 1646) drawn from Greco-Roman law, the dual vornicii of
# Tara de Sus and Tara de Jos, and the post-1711 Phanariot apparatus
# all furnish distinct named institutions across crown, nobles,
# clergy, peasants, and church axes. Six warrant the full slate.

# 1. Sfatul Domnesc -- the Princely Council of great boyars that
#    advised the hospodar and elected new princes from "os de domn"
#    candidates of the ruling family. The council comprised the

#    Vistier (treasurer), Mare Spatar (sword-bearer and military
#    commander), and Mare Postelnic, drawn from the great families
#    whose genealogies Nicolae Stoicescu traced through the
#    fifteenth-century rolls. Stephen the Great's council retained
#    over 110 boyars across his reign of 1457-1504, binding crown

# 2. Mitropolia of Suceava -- the autonomous Moldavian see established
#    in 1386 and recognised in 1401 by the Ecumenical Patriarchate
#    under Joseph, with three suffragan bishoprics and jurisdiction
#    over the entire principality. The metropolitan crowned the
#    hospodar, supplied literate clerks to the chancery from the
#    Putna and Neamt monastery scriptoria, and translated patriarchal
#    canons for use in Moldavian courts. The seat moved permanently
#    from Suceava to Iasi in 1677, but the institution's continuity

# 3. Razesi Free Villages -- the landed free peasantry of Moldavia,
#    organised as obste village communities led by a cneaz and

#    Stephen the Great drew the oastea cea mica from these freeholders,
#    raising voinici infantry and hansari light cavalry directly from

#    boyar retinues and supplied the social base that made Moldavian

# 4. Pravila of Vasile Lupu -- the Carte romaneasca de invatatura
#    promulgated at Iasi in 1646, the first printed legal code in
#    Moldavia and the first official codification of civil law
#    composed in Romanian using Cyrillic script. Compiled under
#    Vasile Lupu (1634-1653) by the logothete Eustratie, the Pravila
#    drew on the Leges Coloniariae of Justinian II, Leunclavius's
#    Jus Graeco-Romanum (Frankfurt 1596), and Farinacci's Praxis et
#    theoricae criminalis (Venice 1610), supplementing them with
#    Moldavian customary norms on land, debt, and crime. Issued from
#    the Trei Ierarhi monastery press, the code bound the hospodar's
#    courts to written procedure and gave Moldavian justice a
#    juridical authority that survived the Phanariot interlude.
