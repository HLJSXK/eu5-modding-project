#    descendants (1370-1592), who ruled Biapish from Lahijan as Twelver

#    The Karkiya combined the dynastic legitimacy of the Sayyid lineage
#    with the religious authority of the Imami clergy: every contract,
#    grant, and military commission ran through the household whose blood
#    sanctified it. The arrangement made Lahijan a refuge for Shia ulama

# 3. Sefidrud Rice Cadaster -- the land-and-water register of the Sefidrud
#    delta paddy fields, the agricultural heart of Gilan. Surveyed in jiribs,
#    annotated for water rights along the qanats and weirs feeding each
#    field, and updated each season by the kadkhuda village chiefs reporting
#    to Rasht and Lahijan, the cadaster fixed the rice tribute owed by every
#    village and the sharecropping rates between landlord and cultivator.
#    Gilan's wet-rice surplus fed the Caspian rim from Tabriz to Astrakhan;
#    the cadaster was the technical instrument that made the surplus
#    extractable. Decay meant either flooded fields nobody owned or
#    landlords seizing water rights upstream of the peasants below.
